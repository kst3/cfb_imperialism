# College Football Imperialism

Production domain: `https://collegefootballimperialism.com/`. The game canonical URLs use this domain. Publish the root site files to the domain's document root; DNS and HTTPS are configured with the domain/hosting provider. Existing internal save keys retain the original Saturday/Sunday Empire names to preserve campaign compatibility.

Open `index.html` for college football or `nfl.html` for NFL. Settings contains edition and legal links. Legal links open an accessible overlay using `legal-ui.js`; the same documents remain directly available at their own URLs. The legal pages use `site.css`. Complete the marked operator and deployment details in `legal.html` and `privacy.html` before publishing; the legal drafts describe US hosting, with operator location and applicable state/international requirements still to be confirmed.

The separate NFL edition is **`nfl.html`** (Sunday Empire). It uses `nfl-data.js` plus the same bundled map, styles and game engine. Upload both new files alongside the existing root files. Its 32-team roster follows [NFL's team directory](https://www.nfl.com/teams/). NFL saves and settings are separate from college campaigns.

NFL starting territories give each state with exactly one home-stadium team entirely to that team. States with multiple teams are divided among their home teams using the same in-state proximity logic as the CFB edition. Home-stadium states place the Giants and Jets in New Jersey and the Commanders in Maryland; Buffalo gets all of New York. Other states without teams, including Hawaii, start gray and unclaimed and can be claimed through the original CFB attack rules. Alaska remains excluded. Coordinates are approximate game anchors, not audited stadium coordinates or fan boundaries. The Giants/Jets use distinct nearby New Jersey anchors so both receive land despite sharing a stadium. The Rams and Chargers anchors share a longitude, with the Rams north of Los Angeles and the Chargers south, creating a north/south split. New England owns MA, ME, NH, VT, RI and CT; Carolina owns NC and SC; Kansas City owns MO and KS. The Commanders also start with DC, alongside their share of Maryland. Florida uses the Oklahoma connectivity repair to transfer detached mainland pieces to a touching territory, with repaired geometry used for attacks as well as rendering. NFL rankings and their setting are removed. NFL map logos have no circular background and scale up to 52 screen pixels, with collision spacing for nearby teams. Hover labels include team cities and states. This layout uses a new NFL save slot; earlier NFL saves are left intact in the old slot. Run `nfl.html?test=1` for NFL geometry and gameplay checks.

A small browser-based college football imperialism game. **Double-click `index.html` to play.** No build, server, Node.js, API key, or installation is needed. Internet access loads ESPN logos and optional Google Fonts; the map and game work offline; logos require a connection.

## Uploading and development files

Upload the root HTML, CSS and JavaScript files (including `awards.js`), and the `LICENSES/` directory to a static web host. No build step is required. Exclude `dev/` and `.gitignore` from the upload.

All development materials live in `dev/`: this README, `tests.js`, the editable `locations.csv`, `tools/`, and saved browser profiles and logs in `artifacts/`. Commands below run from the project root; open the root `index.html` to play or run tests. Keep `dev/` locally to use `index.html?test=1`.
## Implemented

- **Free real estate** rewards two personal claims of previously unowned territories; conquered land and inherited claims never count. **Pacific Hoarder** is limited to teams from California, Oregon, Washington, or Hawaii and requires simultaneous ownership of every territory in California, Oregon, Washington, and Hawaii, including inland cells. Both awards support history replay and undo, and stay with their earners after elimination.

- CFB awards appear on team cards and in the **trophy button below the leaderboard icon**, with requirements, recipients and the round earned. Campaign awards use trophy icons and larger cards; rivalry wins use flags and compact rows. Selected teams list each earned campaign award and rivalry win by name. A brief announcement follows the capture animation. Earned awards remain with their original team after elimination; reload and undo replay history, and reset restores starting awards. NFL has no college awards.
- **Perfect Season** requires 12 opponent victories, following the [NCAA regular-season limit](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/bylaw?refDate=20260927&ruleId=33); neutral claims do not count. **Don't mind me** rewards zero-win survivors when exactly 5 teams remain, regardless of land-area rank. **Represent [conference]** rewards the last surviving member of a 2026 football conference, excluding independents. Optional FCS teams use their own conferences; a sole selected representative qualifies at the start.
- **Lone Star** requires a Texas-based team to own every Texas starting cell. **President** rewards only the original neutral DC claim, never a later conquest. **Coast to Coast** rewards only the first team to achieve simultaneous ownership of Atlantic and Pacific shoreline cells, derived from exterior map arcs, with geographic filters excluding the Great Lakes, Gulf coast and international land borders. Hawaii does not count as the West Coast. Shoreline classification uses the bundled simplified map, not county boundaries.
- Rivalry data in `awards.js` covers named head-to-head games and trophy matchups between the base FBS programs, including dormant rivalries. One award uses the game nickname or named trophy, with matchup IDs distinguishing repeated names such as Battle for the Bell. Captured opponents' prior victories never count. Multi-team series awards are excluded. Data references: [rivalry inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NCAA_college_football_rivalry_games), [NCAA trophy games](https://s3.amazonaws.com/fs.ncaa.org/Docs/stats/football_records/FBS.pdf), [2026 programs and conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_I_FBS_football_programs), [Sacramento State's MAC announcement](https://hornetsports.com/news/2026/2/16/hornet-football-to-join-the-mac-in-2026.aspx), [Mountain West 2026 schedule](https://themw.com/news/2026/03/09/mw-announces-2026-football-schedule/), [Louisiana Tech joins Sun Belt](https://sunbeltsports.org/news/2026/7/1/general-sun-belt-officially-welcomes-louisiana-tech.aspx), [Civil ConFLiCT](https://ucfknights.com/news/2021/11/15/game-notes-ucf-vs-uconn), and [Texas Shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor%E2%80%93Texas_Tech_football_rivalry). This is a bundled catalog; changing conference memberships or rivalry names requires updating it.

- Before the first result, CFB Settings offers **Add FCS teams to empty states** (off by default): Maine, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, South Dakota State, Montana, and Montana State. Only states absent from the base roster are eligible, with two Montana rivals and one team per other state. Vermont remains unclaimed. These are curated representatives, not a live ranking; the [NCAA FCS poll](https://www.ncaa.com/rankings/football/fcs/stats-perform-fcs-top-25) provides ranking context. The map rebuilds immediately, saves the choice with the campaign, and hides the toggle after a result (including an automatic neutral claim). Reset retains the choice and makes the toggle available again. Existing FBS saves retain their progress, and NFL is unaffected.

- The contiguous US and a compact, unlabeled Hawaii inset southwest of the mainland. Alaska is excluded. State lines remain visible across merged empires and can be toggled in Settings.
- 138 programs on the 2026 FBS roster, including transitioning programs, with logos and hometown markers.
- State-first starting territories: split a state between its teams using a projected Voronoi diagram with gently wavy shared edges. State coastlines and borders remain unchanged. A single team gets its whole state. Teamless states start gray and unclaimed, including Montana. An arrow hitting unclaimed land first automatically awards the whole state to the attacking team without a winner dialog.
- One floating button draws a random team through a sliding logo reel with a random landing point inside the winning card and a prolonged slowdown, then waits for another click to spin its attack direction. The map focuses on the selected team. A persistent top panel shows the attacker and adds the defender when the arrow lands. Click the selected team again or click the map background to deselect it and draw another.
- Geometric first-target detection, including unclaimed states. You can also click a team on the map and use the same button to spin, or click a second team for a manual matchup.
- The arrow spins at a compact size and extends to the first target. After landing, the map frames its origin and landing point and waits for the user to click the winning team in the top matchup preview. Both preview cards become keyboard-accessible winner buttons once both teams are selected. Choosing one hides the arrow. The winner takes the loser's accumulated empire with an irregular flood-like color reveal. Clicking the map background clears an unresolved matchup. Automatic claims use the same capture animation. Reduced-motion settings skip the animations. The reveal removes the old fill without an old-color stroke, exposing the merged winner territory underneath. Routine notifications are announced to screen readers without appearing above the bottom button.
- Full-viewport map, wheel zoom without page scrolling, drag/pinch navigation, saved progress, champion state, and reduced-motion support. Campaign history and undo remain in the game engine; their visible panels and controls are removed.

## Technology and rules

Selected teams show campaign wins below their name. Opponent victories count as wins; neutral claims do not. The top 25 surviving teams by current displayed land area have a brush-style #1–#25 prefix on team cards. Area includes captured and claimed land and excludes holes, using the displayed projections (including Hawaii's illustrative inset). Ties break alphabetically. Statistics rebuild from history and ownership, supporting existing saves, undo, and reset.

Starting territory shapes are generated deterministically from the team locations and state outlines. They are not randomly redrawn between campaigns: the same inputs and projection produce the same starting shapes. Oklahoma has a connectivity repair that assigns OU's detached western panhandle fragment to the touching Oklahoma State region, keeping all three starting territories connected. Both rendering and attack detection use the repaired geometry.

The map fills the browser viewport with no header, footer, sidebar, or document scrolling. The round button and a small top-right settings cog remain visible when idle. Settings offers a confirmed reset that clears campaign progress and restores the starting map view. Markers display logos without abbreviation text; territories use their stored team color directly.

Drawing or selecting a team automatically fits the camera to its current territory, including conquered and claimed land. Small territories receive a closer view; larger empires zoom out, with space around the territory for neighboring teams. The direction spin starts from this same territory view.
Once an opponent is selected or the arrow lands on one, the camera fits both teams' full territories with a tighter margin to keep the matchup easy to see.

Plain **JavaScript + SVG**, with vendored D3 7 and TopoJSON Client 3. This is a good starting point for a small game: editable files, accurate map boundaries, no framework setup. TypeScript with Vite would be a useful later upgrade as the game grows.

Original starting cells retain an owner ID; a conquest updates every cell owned by the loser. The renderer clips starting cells to the state coastline, then unions all land held by each surviving team into a single SVG path. Adjacent pieces lose their shared edge; disconnected pieces stay disconnected. State lines stay visible independently of territory ownership. The Settings toggle shows or hides all state lines and remembers the preference separately from campaign progress. Undo and saved campaigns rebuild the same merged geometry.

Territories have a thin stroke in their own fill color to cover antialiasing seams between adjacent SVG paths. The stroke stays the same screen width at every zoom level; it does not change ownership geometry or hit detection.

The attack ray starts at the surviving team's original hometown. It follows a straight line on the **displayed map**, rather than a geographic compass bearing or great-circle route. Hawaii uses a separate conic projection placed southwest of the mainland; inset size and distance are illustrative. Ray/edge intersections and midpoint containment determine the first opposing or unclaimed land, skipping water and friendly territory. Unclaimed land is awarded immediately and subsequently transfers with its owner's empire. Claims support save validation and undo; existing saved campaigns are migrated without losing progress. Spins without a target are rerolled. After 256 misses, a direction toward a rival hometown is used; manual selection remains available if no hit is found.

Older saves containing Alaska are replayed without Alaska claims, preserving other claims and conquests. The Alaska removal and save migration option has been removed.

## Data and accuracy

Texas Tech's territory color uses official scarlet `#E90802`, verified against the [Texas Tech visual identity guidelines](https://www.ttu.edu/brand/visual-identity/index.php) on September 30, 2026. The guidelines also specify black `#000000` and white `#FFFFFF` as primary colors.

`dev/locations.csv` is the editable location inventory. Coordinates represent campus or stadium points in each hometown, **not independently verified exact locations**. In particular, off-campus stadium programs can use campus locations to keep hometowns meaningful. Further coordinate auditing is needed before claiming exact placement.

`data.js` bundles team metadata and map topology so `file://` opening works. Runtime reads `data.js`; after editing `dev/locations.csv`, run `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File dev/tools/update-locations.ps1` to copy changes into the bundle. Team IDs are ESPN IDs. Adding/removing programs also requires updating bundled team metadata and the roster count in the tests.

Sources:

- US Census-derived boundaries: [US Atlas 3](https://github.com/topojson/us-atlas), `states-10m.json` (ISC).
- Team names, colors, IDs, and logos: [ESPN college football teams endpoint](https://site.api.espn.com/apis/site/v2/sports/football/college-football/teams?limit=1000). Logo marks belong to their respective owners; this is an unofficial fan project.
- [NCAA realignment coverage](https://www.ncaa.com/news/football/article/2026-04-03/making-sense-fcs-conference-realignment) and [2026 FBS roster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_I_FBS_football_programs).
- [Polygon Clipping 0.15.7](https://github.com/mfogel/polygon-clipping) (MIT), bundled locally for territory unions; see `LICENSES/polygon-clipping.txt`.
- [D3](https://github.com/d3/d3) (ISC) and [TopoJSON Client](https://github.com/topojson/topojson-client) (ISC); see `LICENSES/`.

Territories use map-plane proximity within states, not county borders. Shared territory edges use deterministic waves, clipped to each state, with the same geometry used for rendering and arrow detection. Logo badges are up to 30 pixels wide and keep a fixed screen size while zooming, with a smaller size on narrow screens. Crowded badges move only a limited distance from their hometowns: up to three quarters of a badge radius at overview zoom, and up to one and a half radii when zoomed in. Some overlap is allowed to preserve location accuracy. Layout is cached while panning; the marker layer moves with the map. Placement updates when zooming, resizing, or changing surviving teams; attack origins remain at the original hometowns. Hover does not highlight territories. Selected territories share an outer border and glow that fade in and out over 240 ms; the combined effect fades away after a capture. Clicking the selected defender again removes it from the matchup while keeping the attacker selected.

## Checks

Open `index.html?test=1` to run browser tests (append `?test=1` to the local file URL). Tests use fresh state and never read/write the player's saved campaign. Results appear at the top of the page. They cover roster integrity, hometown ownership, state coverage without gaps or overlaps, chain transfers, save validation, undo, narrow targets, water, clipping, Hawaii, automatic claims, Alaska removal and save migration, reset confirmation, state lines, logo spacing at maximum zoom, background deselection, camera focus, the sliding draw, landing confirmation, accessible winner selection, and the capture animation.

Progress uses `localStorage` with history replay validation. Browsers may restrict storage for local files; a visible message indicates unavailable storage. Keep the same file path/browser to retain a campaign. Clearing browser data removes the save.
